What these four levels mean

Errors and warnings are about the wiki as a structure — whether it holds together and can be navigated. They say nothing about whether the content is true.

Errors
The build is broken: a dead [[wikilink]], a missing frontmatter field, a duplicate id, a cluster with no hub, or a router index / this viewer out of sync with the markdown. validate.py exits 1.
Warnings
Intact but degraded: an orphan nothing links to, a description too thin to route on, an ambiguous link, a document that reached no node. Fails the gate only under --strict.
Conflicts
Two sources disagree, or one source contradicts itself. Recorded, never resolved — the compiler may not pick a winner, average two numbers, or prefer the newer file.
Notes
Everything else worth knowing: an unsupported document, a check that could not run.

Conflicts are filed by validate.py at info severity and promoted to their own level here. That is deliberate: a conflict is a fact about your source documents, not a defect in the wiki. Capturing one is the compiler working correctly, so it can never be an error.

Zero errors and zero warnings is not a clean bill of health. Open conflicts, low-confidence nodes and images dropped during extraction all sit underneath a passing gate — those are reported by the audit, not by validation.

        Why this is not the number of conflicts
        • It counts pairs of source documents, not conflicts and not lines on the graph. One pair is listed once however many times those two documents disagree.
        • A conflict where a single document contradicts itself has no second document to pair with, so it appears in Problems and contributes nothing here.
        • Smaller again is the number of contradicts lines on the graph. That is an edge between two nodes, so it exists only when each side was written up on its own page. Most conflicts are documented on one page describing both sides — those carry a conflict flag, not a line.
